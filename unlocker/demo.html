Grandmother's Lemon Cake

A plain loaf, soaked in lemon syrup while it's still warm. It keeps for a week and gets better every day.

By Ada Fennimore · Baking · 12 minutes to read

My grandmother made this cake on Sundays, in a tin so dented it could only have been older than she was. She never wrote the recipe down. When I finally stood next to her with a notebook, she measured everything with the same chipped teacup, so the quantities below are my best translation of teacups into grams.

The method is the old one for a reason. Creaming the butter and sugar for a full five minutes is what makes the crumb light; the syrup, poured over the hot cake, is what makes it keep.

Ingredients

  • 175 g soft butter and 175 g caster sugar
  • 3 eggs, at room temperature
  • 175 g self-raising flour and a pinch of salt
  • The zest of 2 lemons, and 4 tablespoons of milk
  • For the syrup: the juice of 2 lemons and 100 g granulated sugar

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 180 °C (160 °C fan). Line a 900 g loaf tin with baking paper.
  2. Beat the butter and sugar until pale and fluffy, a good five minutes. Beat in the eggs one at a time.
  3. Fold in the flour, salt and zest, then the milk. Scrape into the tin and bake for 45 to 50 minutes, until a skewer comes out clean.
  4. While it bakes, stir the lemon juice and granulated sugar together. Don't let it dissolve: the crunch is the point.
  5. Prick the hot cake all over and spoon the syrup over it. Leave it in the tin until completely cold.
