Privacy Policy

Effective 10 November 2026

In short: Multitool and its Safari extensions do not collect, share or sell any personal data. Each tool works in Safari on your own device, and keeps only its settings and what you choose to save. Nothing is sent to the developer or to anyone else.

Who we are

Multitool for Safari is published by Dmitry Skorinko, an individual developer, through the App Store.

Everything at once

  • No collection. The app and its extensions contain no analytics, advertising, tracking or crash-reporting code, and make no requests to the developer or to any third party.
  • On your device. What a tool stores is kept in Safari’s storage for that extension, on your device. Removing the app removes everything it stored.
  • You choose where. Each tool is a separate Safari extension. You turn on only the ones you want, and Safari asks you before any of them may run on your websites. You can allow a tool on every website, on chosen sites only, or turn it off at any time in Safari’s settings.
  • Nothing leaves unless you send it. Things leave your device only when you save, copy, share or export them yourself.

The Multitool app

The app shows the tools, how to turn them on, and their in-app purchases. It stores which tools you have unlocked, on your device, so that the extensions can check it there. That check never leaves your device.

Purchases

Paid tools are unlocked through in-app purchases in the App Store. Apple handles payment, and the developer does not receive your name, email address or payment details. Apple’s own privacy policy applies to purchases.

Each tool

JSON Prettifier

By default it has no access to your websites: Safari lets it into a page only when you click its toolbar button, choose it in the page menu, press its keyboard shortcut or use its context menu, and only into that page. If you turn on automatic formatting, Safari asks you to give it access to websites; it then checks each page only for whether Safari shows it as JSON text, and leaves other pages as they are. It reads a JSON page’s text to draw it as a tree and doesn’t keep it after you close the page. It stores your settings (theme, text size, sites where automatic formatting is off), and text you send to the Scratchpad only until the Scratchpad opens it. Image previews are off unless you turn them on; then Safari loads an image from its address when you point at it, without telling that site which page you came from. JSON Web Tokens are decoded on your device.

Full Page Screenshot

It captures a page only when you ask (toolbar button, keyboard shortcut or right-click menu), and Safari then gives it access to that one page, in that one tab. To capture the whole page it scrolls it and takes a picture of each screen, then puts the page back. It doesn’t read or keep the page’s text. Screenshots are kept in the extension’s storage on your device so the editor can show them, and deleted after a day; your options (file format, resolution) are kept there too.

Page Unlocker

So that it can turn right-click, copy, selection, paste and printing back on, it runs on the pages you open, on the websites you allow in Safari. It changes how the page handles those actions and counts what the page tried to block, so its toolbar button can show you; the counts are kept only while the page is open. It doesn’t read or keep the page’s text and doesn’t record which pages you visit. Copy Page Text reads the page’s text only when you click it, and puts it on your clipboard. It stores your choices: the mode for each site, sites where the page’s own right-click menu should show, and your settings.

Text Highlighter

So that your highlights reappear when you come back to a page, it runs on the pages you open, on the websites you allow, and looks only for the text of your own highlights. When you highlight text it stores the highlighted words and a few words around them, your note if you write one, the colour, the date, and the page’s address and title. Your settings are stored too.

Dark Mode

To put a website in dark mode it reads the page’s styles and colours and adds its own styles on top, only on sites where you allow it. It doesn’t read what you type or record which sites you visit. Safari doesn’t let an extension read a stylesheet or picture that a page loads from another address, so to recolour those it downloads the same file again, from the address the page uses, without your cookies. It stores your settings (when websites go dark, brightness, photo dimming), your choices for individual sites and fixes you made, which sites were found to be dark already, and a copy of the dark theme for recent sites so the next visit starts dark at once (up to 80 sites, each for at most 14 days). Report this site opens a draft email in your mail app with the page’s address (without anything after a ? or #), your Dark Mode setting and the version numbers; nothing is sent unless you send it.

Color Picker

It asks for no access to your websites: Safari lets it into a page only when you click its toolbar button, choose it in the page menu or press its keyboard shortcut. While you pick, it reads the colours of the page’s elements and takes a picture of the visible part of the page to show the pixels under the pointer; the picture stays in memory on the page and is gone when you stop. For each colour you pick it stores the colour, the time and the website’s name (such as example.com, not the full address), up to your last 300 picks, plus the palettes you save and your settings.

Page Extractor

It reads a page only when you ask (toolbar button, keyboard shortcut or right-click menu), and Safari then gives it access to that one page, in that one tab. It lists the page’s links, email addresses and pictures and shows them to you; that list is kept in the extension’s storage for up to a day so the results tab can show it. Pictures in the results load from their own sites, as they would on the page. When you choose Download ZIP, Safari asks whether it may fetch the selected pictures from their sites; it then downloads them directly into a file on your device. It stores your preferences: copy format, templates, picture size and the filters you last used on each website.

Markdown Viewer

It runs only on pages whose address ends in .md, .markdown, .mdown or .mkd, reads the Markdown text Safari has already loaded, and shows it formatted. It requests the page you are viewing again, from the same address, in two cases: when the server doesn’t state a text encoding, and for live reload on local addresses such as localhost. Images and links inside a file load from wherever the file points, as on any web page. It stores two settings: whether formatting is on, and whether the contents sidebar was left open.

Stop All Scripts

On the websites where you turn JavaScript off, it tells Safari not to run the page’s scripts, shows what the site provides for browsers without JavaScript, loads pictures that would wait for a script, and counts the scripts it stopped. It needs access to the websites you visit so it can stop scripts before the first one runs. It doesn’t keep page content or record which pages you visit. It stores the list of websites where you turned JavaScript off (or the websites you trust), the choices you made for each, and your settings.

Children

Multitool collects no data from anyone, including children.
